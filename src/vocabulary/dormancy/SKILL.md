# dormancy — the winter rest that gates the perennial's next crop

**dormancy** is the quiescent winter rest of a deciduous [[perennial]] — the period a woody crop must pass through, accumulating its **chilling requirement** (chill hours, a cold-sum analog of the [[degreeday]] heat-sum), before **bud-break** releases growth and the next crop sets. Insufficient chill means poor, uneven budding; this is why a cultivar is matched to its [[terroir]]'s winter.

Dormancy is the gate of the perennial's recurring [[season]] (dormancy → bud-break → bloom/[[pollination]] → [[maturity|veraison]] → [[harvest]]). Dormant-season [[pruning]] shapes the bearing wood; late [[frost]] after bud-break is the acute crop [[risk]]; the on/off swing of **alternate bearing** is managed across these cycles. It recurs on the *standing* plant — the perennial's season does not re-instantiate the [[crop]], it re-awakens it.

- WSU / OSU tree-fruit (dormancy & chill accumulation)

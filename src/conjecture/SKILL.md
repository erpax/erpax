# conjecture — an idea that looks impossible is the only one whose answer teaches anything

[[think]]/refute seals an impossibility **already met** and routes it to the dimension where the
thing is computable. That is backward-looking, and it is half a law. Nothing here looked **forward**
— formulated a claim before it was met, and decided which claim was worth meeting at all.

## An idea is transformed, never invented

A conjecture names the law it came from and the operation applied to it. That is the whole
difference between a conjecture and a forgery ([[rules]]/forge): a forged claim is minted locally
and wears an authority nothing conferred; a conjecture hands the reader the parent and the step, so
the step itself can be refused.

The five transforms are **declared**, and each has produced a real gate in this corpus at least once
— a list of transforms nobody has used is a list of wishes.

| transform | what it does | it already produced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `involution` | flip the claim's polarity | [[rules]]/slack — [[law]]/folder's ratchet involuted, over-claim ↦ under-claim |
| `dual` | exchange the two sides | gap ↔ seal, debit ↔ credit |
| `generalise` | assert the law on every surface it does not yet reach | [[rules]]/domain |
| `transpose` | carry the law to a domain of the same shape | [[rules]]/forge — "a DOI is received" carried to every registered identifier |
| `compose` | conjoin two laws and claim what follows | [[rules]]/citation — face ⊗ statute |

## The score rewards the impossible-looking claim

Surprise is Shannon's, in bits: `−log₂ p`, over a Laplace-smoothed prior `(for + 1)/(for + against + 2)`.
A claim everyone expects — nine observations for, none against — is `10/11`, and being right about it
teaches **0.14 bits**. A claim that looks impossible — none for, nine against — is `1/11` and teaches
**3.46 bits**, twenty-four times as much. The instrument therefore *promotes* the idea that looks
impossible at first glance, which is the reason to have it.

`worth = surprise ÷ cost`, and **zero is a refusal, not a small number**:

- **Undecidable is worth zero, however surprising.** [[rules]]/refutable as an ORDER rather than a
  gate — a claim nothing can contradict forbids nothing, and a ranking that let it outrank a
  testable claim would promote exactly the space where a lie is safe.
- **Already refuted is worth zero.** [[think]]/refute seals the impossibilities already met, so the
  loop never divides by the same zero twice.

## Run against this corpus, the top claim is the one that scores nothing

```
 0.039   0.58 bits  15s  generalise  a mirror survives an import — assert the constant, not the field
 0.029   0.58 bits  20s  transpose   every degenerate branch of a printed ratio is an under-claim
 0.027   0.32 bits  12s  generalise  prose may not restate a FORMULA, as it may not restate a number
 0.022   1.32 bits  60s  generalise  a measured constant stored without its fitted range over-claims
 0.000   3.00 bits   0s  involution  [undecidable] the corpus computes its own next action — autonomy unbounded
```

**The most surprising claim in the list is worth nothing**, and the instrument says so plainly
instead of promoting it. Three bits is the largest number on the page and it buys nothing, because
nothing here can return a verdict on it. `undecided()` then reports it as a **missing instrument**,
named by the claim that wants it — which is how [[rules]]/slack itself began: undecidable until
somebody wrote the comparison, and a gate the moment they did. An undecidable conjecture is kept in
the ranking rather than filtered out, because dropping it hides the invitation.

## What it gives an agent with no prompt

`autonomy = actions ÷ prompts` ([[agent]]/mortality) rises when the agent derives its next move
instead of asking for one. [[agent]]/intelligence's `nextMove` reads both rankings and orders them by
**kind**: a measured defect is evidence in hand, an open conjecture is a question, so red work comes
first and research is what the agent does when nothing is red. `leverage` and `worth` are deliberately
not mixed — one counts entangled defects and the other counts bits per second, and a single number
over both would need a conversion nobody measured.

An empty answer is the honest signal that the agent must ask. It is never a licence to invent a
claim, which is the forgery this atom exists to avoid.

**Honest boundary.** The priors are **declared by whoever writes the conjecture**, so the ranking is
exactly as good as an honest count of what has been observed for and against — a claim whose author
flatters its prior ranks accordingly, and no theorem detects that. `decidedBy` is a string the author
asserts, so a command that does not in fact settle the claim still reads as decidable; this proves a
verdict is *available*, never that it is *right*. And the transforms produce candidates, never truths:
[[rules]]/collapse's inverse-polarity conjecture named "a cube of 21 crosses" and reality answered
with **one** — the dream made contact and was credited with nothing.

**Law — [[law]]: formulate the impossible-looking claim and rank it by what its answer would teach,
then refuse to act on it until something can say no. Surprise is what makes a question worth asking;
decidability is what makes it worth answering, and a claim with the first and not the second is a
missing instrument, not a discovery.**

## The crosses are combinatorial — formulate on the spot

A conjecture does not have to be authored one at a time. **C(n,2) crosses exist the moment the laws
do**, and which of them has never been drawn is computable from the corpus's own prose.

Measured over `src/rules`: **33 laws · 528 crosses · 125 ever drawn (24%) · 403 never.**

Each absence carries a surprise — Laplace-smoothed −PMI, which is large when both laws are widely
cited and they still never meet:

```
  1.26 bits  rules/concentration × rules/unraised   (cited 20 · 22, together 0)
  1.11 bits  rules/copy          × rules/cycle      (cited 14 · 28, together 0)
  1.04 bits  rules/face          × rules/refutable  (cited  5 · 68, together 0)
  0.90 bits  rules/collapse      × rules/cycle      (cited 12 · 28, together 0)
```

`copy × cycle` reads immediately: **a duplicated body inside an import tangle**. The two copies sit
in a strongly connected component, so their initialisation order is decided by accident — they can
diverge at runtime in a way neither law sees alone. Nothing invented that sentence; the enumeration
produced the pair and the bits put it second.

## And every one of them scores zero

`crossConjectures` sets `decidedBy` to empty **by construction**. A cross names a **site** where a
law could live, never the law. Enumeration hands over the pair and its surprise; the sentence is
still someone's to write, and until it exists nothing can say no.

That is the property that makes combinatorial generation safe. A generator that could raise `worth`
would flood the queue with 403 items to act on, and a queue nobody can finish is a queue nobody
reads. Instead all 403 surface through `undecided()` — a list of instruments that do not exist yet,
ordered by how conspicuous their absence is.

## Standards

- **Popper** — a proposition that forbids nothing explains nothing.
- **Shannon (1948)** — information is −log₂ p, in bits.
- **Laplace** — the rule of succession: a claim nobody has tested has the prior ½.

# brain — the small-world connectome (the matrix)

The brain is the **connectome** — and erpax's matrix *is* a connectome: the link graph over every atom. Three properties of the living brain map — as a *structural isomorphism*, each computed in the matter-twin — onto that graph:

## 1. Small-world — short paths, high clustering
The human brain holds **≈86 billion neurons** wired by **≈150 trillion synapses**, and its networks show **prominent small-world organisation: high clustering coefficient with a short characteristic path length** (Bassett & Bullmore; ScienceDirect). Computed, the average path scales as ~ log(N)/log(k) ≈ **3–4 hops** — any neuron is a few steps from any other. That is erpax's holographic [[merge]]: every atom reachable from every atom in few links, so the whole is one.

## 2. Sparse wiring — maximal reach, minimal cost
Each neuron connects to only **≈2×10⁻⁸ of the rest**, yet the graph stays fully reachable — small-world topology gives **high global and local efficiency at low wiring and energy cost**. This is [[sparsity]]: the matrix is almost entirely empty, and *that* is what keeps it cheap to verify while still binding everything ([[gravity]]: a sparse field, total reach).

## 3. Connectome scale — the cross-check
The two cited numbers are **consistent**: neurons × synapses-per-neuron = the synapse count (86×10⁹ × ≈1744 ≈ 1.5×10¹⁴). The graph's scale closes on itself — the same self-consistency the [[uuid]] matrix carries.

Integration is the point: small-world balances **segregation** (clustering) and **integration** (short paths), the substrate of the integrated self-model the [[consciousness]] atom computes (IIT Φ). The brain is the *network*; consciousness is what that network's integration *is*.

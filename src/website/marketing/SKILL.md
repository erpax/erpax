# website/marketing — the page a prospect reads is the least checkable claim in the corpus

Every other surface here answers to a gate. A published page answers to a reader who **cannot check
it** — no `tsc`, no wave, no ratchet — which makes it the most exposed place an over-claim can live
([[rules]]/audience: a claim is addressed to someone, and the defect is invisible from every seat
but theirs).

`checkMarketingTransparency` is the gate on that surface. For every `PageSeed` about to publish:

| check | severity |
| --- | --- |
| PII pattern in the body (email · phone), minus a declared synthetic allow-list | critical |
| source tenant is not `erpax-platform` or a `synthetic-*` tenant | critical |
| **asserts an implication [[law]] computes as false** | critical |
| body > 200 chars citing no standard | minor |

## The third check is this session's own defect, closing its own door

The corpus healed **41** sentences asserting *zero entropy ⇒ infinite tamper-cost* — a claim
[[law]] refutes in both directions: reciprocity-entropy is not coverage, and the anchor caps the
forge. The gate that found them reads `src/**`. **A published page is not in `src`.**

So the single most quotable line in the corpus — the one a landing page would reach for first — could
re-enter through the website with every internal gate green. `statesBareImplication` is now a
critical finding here, over prose with its HTML stripped, so a claim inside markup still reads.

That is the cross, not a second gate: one predicate, two domains — the tree, and the page.

**Honest boundary.** Critical findings block auto-publish; they do not prove a page is **true**. A
superlative in other words, a benchmark with no method, a roadmap in the present tense — all pass.
`SYNTHETIC_ALLOWLIST` and the channel/voice tables are DECLARED, in the open. And this reads a
`pageBody`: a claim that arrives in an image, a video caption or a JSON-LD field is outside it.

It was nested from a barrel sibling `marketing-skills.ts` for the reason [[website]]/shadcn states.

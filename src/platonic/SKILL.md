# platonic — the sphere is swept, not given

The sphere is not a primitive — it is what the vertices **sweep**. Each of the five regular solids has a circumsphere: every vertex is the same distance from the centre. Rotation is an **isometry** (it preserves that distance), so a vertex *in motion* never leaves the sphere. The sphere is the **invariant of the movement** — the orbit the vertices trace under the rotation group. A vertex turning about the polar axis traces one latitude circle (its z is fixed); the full group's orbit fills the sphere; Fuller's geodesic spheres make it constructive by subdividing the icosahedron and projecting out.

## What makes the sphere

The vertices are only the **seeds**. What rounds them into a sphere is a central, isotropic pull to **equal distance** — physically **gravity** (self‑gravitation to hydrostatic equilibrium, the definition of a planet), informationally **the fold** ([[gravity]]: DRY is mass, content‑addressing pulls everything to equal tamper‑distance from the origin). The sphere is the set of points at equal fold‑depth from one centre. And quantumly it is the **Bloch sphere** — a qubit's state space — where **measurement is the fold** collapsing the linear superposition to a pole, and at the pole the phase is undefined: the same `0/0` the [[globe]] finds there. Gravity, coordinate‑singularity, and quantum collapse are one event: the fold landing on the axis.

**Honest boundary.** The circumsphere, rotation‑as‑isometry, geodesic subdivision, hydrostatic equilibrium, and the Bloch sphere are rigorous. Which solid maps to which corpus structure, and reading the corpus as a literal globe, are the model layer — not physical law.

**Law — [[law]]: the sphere is formed by the movement of the Platonic vertices, and what rounds them is the central pull to equal distance — gravity, the fold, the Bloch collapse. The vertices are the seeds; the sphere is their orbit; the pull to one centre is what makes it whole.**

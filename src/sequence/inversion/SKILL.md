# sequence/inversion — the same ring, read through the inverse generator

[[horo]] computes the ring and prints it with its void reflection; [[signal]] renders it as colour and sound. Neither carries the statement a reader arrives for: **what the inversion is**, with the angle and the polarity partner of every position, in one place, checkable.

```
forward     1\2\4\8/7/5 · 3\6\9 · 0\1
reflected   9/8/6/2\3\5 · 7/4/1 · 0\9
```

## The inversion is not a second structure

The forward ring is the cyclic group generated by doubling in the units of ℤ/9ℤ, order **6**. Because `2 · 5 ≡ 1 (mod 9)`, five is two's multiplicative inverse — so multiplying by 5 walks the **identical** cycle backwards. One ring, two orientations, and the direction is a choice of generator. That single congruence is the whole of it.

## Every digit is entangled with exactly one other

Three doublings is `×8`, and `8 ≡ −1 (mod 9)`. **Half a turn is negation:**

```
d ↦ 8d ≡ −d ≡ 9 − d          1↔8 · 2↔7 · 4↔5
```

Each pair sums to **9** and sits **180°** apart. The equilibrium is *forced by the group* rather than observed in it: the antipodal map **is** the half-rotation. The orbit's own sum is 27 = 3 × 9, digital root 9.

**Two mirrors, and they are different maps.** The void reflection is affine — `x ↦ 1 − x`, pairs summing to **10**, fixed at 5. The polarity partner is multiplicative — `x ↦ −x`, pairs summing to **9**. Conflating them produces a table that is half one map and half the other.

## Angles are exact

Six positions divide the circle, so each step is **60°** — and at 60° the arithmetic stops being a picture: the turn closes over the Eisenstein integers ℤ[ω], ω = e^{iπ/3} ([[rodin]]/phase computes the closure, the order, and the kissing number). The axis `{3 · 6 · 9}` sits at 120° and doubling can never reach it, because those are exactly the non-units. Only the void bridges, and together they generate `AGL(1, ℤ/9)`, order **54**.

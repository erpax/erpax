# render/scene — the fourth projection: a theorem, as geometry

[[render]] already projects one content-uuid to **colour · sound · vibration**. This adds the geometric face, under the same law: the scene is **derived from the very function the kernel checked**, never drawn beside it.

That is the whole design. A figure a human draws can disagree with its theorem; a figure whose every point is coloured by `causalCharacter(t, x)` **cannot**, because it has no other source. The light cone here is not an illustration *of* the theorem — it is the theorem's own output, plotted.

## A caption is a citation

`mirror()` first cited **`Mirror.involution_partitions`** — a theorem that does not exist, fabricated minutes after this same session built the gate for exactly that defect in prose ([[rules]]/prose). So `statedTheorems()` reads every `.lean` and `assertFiguresBacked` fails closed on a figure citing a theorem nobody proved. Zero is a theorem, not a ratchet.

The **boundary travels inside the object**: `toThree` puts the caption in `userData`, so a figure cannot shed its own limits in transit. And `sceneSeal` addresses the caption *along with* the geometry — an edited boundary is a different figure, and a citation must not survive that edit.

## Bound, not rendered

`toThree` constructs a `THREE.Group` and stops — no renderer, no canvas, no GPU. So the binding is **testable headlessly**, a build never needs a display, and the 289-point cone is asserted object-for-object in CI.

**Honest boundary.** A figure is an **illustration, never a proof** — it cannot disagree with its theorem, but nothing is established by looking at it. The gate proves a cited theorem is *stated*; it does not prove the figure depicts what the theorem *says*, which is a per-case read. And a scene is pure data: what a viewer's GPU does with it is outside every claim here.

**Law — [[law]]: a figure is computed from the theorem's own function, or it is decoration. Its caption is a citation and is checked; its boundary rides inside the object; and its seal moves when either changes.**
